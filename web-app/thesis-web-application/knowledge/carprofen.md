# Carprofen

Carprofen is an anti-inflammatory pain medicine (an NSAID) for dogs. The label is for relief of arthritis pain and for pain after surgery. Prescription only.

## How to give it
- Give it by mouth, with or without food, exactly as your vet told you, for as long as your vet told you.
- If it is stopped or not given as directed, the pain and inflammation may come back.
- Do not add another pain medicine, aspirin, other anti-inflammatories or steroids unless your vet says so. The label says to avoid combining NSAIDs.

## Watch for: stop the medicine and call your vet immediately if you see
- Decreased appetite, vomiting or diarrhea.
- Dark or tarry stools.
- Drinking or urinating more than usual.
- Pale gums, or yellow gums, skin or whites of the eyes.
- Tiredness, poor coordination, seizure or behavior changes.
- The label says serious problems can happen without warning, and rarely can be fatal. Your vet may check blood tests from time to time.

## Do not use if
- Your dog has had an allergic reaction to carprofen, aspirin or another NSAID, or a bad reaction to an NSAID before.
- It is for a cat: carprofen is for dogs only. Call your vet immediately if a cat takes it.
- The label says safety has not been shown in dogs with bleeding disorders, in pregnant, breeding or nursing dogs, or in puppies under 6 weeks.

## Safety
- People must not take dog carprofen. Keep it out of reach of children and call a doctor if someone takes it by accident.
